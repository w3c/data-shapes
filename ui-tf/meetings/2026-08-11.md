# Meeting on 11 August 2026

## Attendees

## Discussion

### Meeting Outcome

* Establish a shared understanding of the points where our expectations differ.
* Reach agreement, where possible, on how the Task Force will work together and how we will approach the SHACL UI specification.
* Clearly record any decisions, unresolved questions, and next steps.

### Task Force Process

This session gives everyone an opportunity to provide their perspective on what they need from the Task Force process.

* Agree on a process for establishing, reviewing, approving, and merging pull requests.
* Agree on how consensus is determined and what happens when consensus cannot be reached.
* Agree on how decisions are recorded so that expectations are clear afterwards.

Some points raised in various discussions and pull requests include:

* when a PR should be held;
* what constitutes a blocking review;
* how much time should be allowed for review; and
* how PRs that depend on unresolved issues or broader discussions should be handled.

### SHACL UI Specification: Conformance, Openness and Interoperability

This session gives everyone in the Task Force an opportunity to provide their perspective on SHACL UI and how prescriptive the specification should be.

* Agree on what conformance to SHACL UI should guarantee and where implementations should retain flexibility.
* Agree on how normative language such as MUST, SHOULD and MAY should be applied.
* Consider mechanisms, such as profiling, for expressing different levels or sets of interoperability requirements.
* Seek to resolve [issue #1083 — *How open/closed should the SHACL UI specification be?*](https://github.com/w3c/data-shapes/issues/1083).

### Second Weekly Meeting

This is a proposal to add a second meeting this week, as our usual Tuesday meeting will be used for the conciliation discussion. We may also consider keeping a second recurring weekly meeting for the remainder of August to help us achieve the document milestone.

## Minutes

Nick noted that the task force needs to complete the content of the document by the end of August.

Ieben and Daniel gave a short presentation summarising issue #1083 and the main arguments raised by both positions. Daniel noted that the presentation was available in the shared task force Google Drive originally created by Edmond.

Bergi said he would prefer to take the issue directly to the Working Group rather than repeat the discussion. Nick noted that the co-chairs were present and suggested proceeding with the presentation for now.

Ieben and Daniel continued the presentation.

### Areas of agreement

The presentation summarised the main arguments raised in the GitHub issue and attempted to identify areas of existing agreement.

Bergi disagreed with point 2, saying that framing the specification as "open and extensible" was too broad and could later be used to justify restrictions he did not support. He suggested reducing the point to: "The specification must be extensible."

Robert said the specification should avoid trying to define every possible behaviour and instead focus on what constitutes a violation of the specification. His intuition was to favour an open-world model.

Daniel clarified that the definitions on the slide were intended to guide the discussion in the meeting rather than appear in the specification itself. He said he believed the group generally agreed that the specification should be extensible, with the remaining question being what should form the core SHACL UI specification.

Eliana asked whether making the specification extensible would require additional work.

Ieben said the current idea was to have a community-maintained set of widgets covering work outside the scope of the task force. Daniel said he believed the task force generally agreed with this direction.

### Narrow normative core or soft core

Robert asked how an implementer could determine whether their implementation renders content consistently with other implementations.

Daniel said conformance would normally be demonstrated through test suites and test cases supplied by the specification. He cautioned against requiring implementation-specific structures, such as a particular JSON representation, merely to make implementations testable.

Bergi said the question was not how something was implemented, but what an implementation was required to implement. He said a conformance model should not exclude otherwise valid use cases, particularly if SHACL UI is intended to become a standard. He also cautioned against assuming web-based implementations and argued that machine testability should not be a hard requirement.

Nick said he understood Daniel's comments as being broadly consistent with Bergi's point, particularly because Daniel had referred to test structures used internally by the task force.

Bergi said the group should be careful with broad statements about machine testability, noting that newer approaches, including AI-based evaluation of screenshots, may offer additional ways of assessing implementations.

Daniel suggested keeping the discussion narrowly focused on the specific issue. He referred to Bergi's example of reordering frequently used fields and said he viewed that kind of behaviour as an innovation layered on top of the specification rather than something the specification should prohibit. He also acknowledged that implementations may not be web-based. He clarified that the proposed test-suite approach did not require testing visual output, but instead proposed a shared data structure for testing behaviour.

Bergi said the current pull request concerning ordering narrowed the range of possible use cases.

Daniel said the intention of the pull request was to support, rather than block, the use case Bergi had described, while acknowledging that it could potentially block unknown use cases.

### `sh:order`

Daniel said he did not agree with framing the discussion as simply open versus closed. His preference was to define the behaviour when `sh:order` is absent, including a fallback tie-breaking algorithm. He described this as option 1.

Daniel also acknowledged that most participants appeared to favour leaving behaviour unspecified when `sh:order` is absent. He said the group had been close to consensus on that approach and described option 2 as a reasonable middle ground.

Ieben said he still preferred option 1 because it provided stronger guarantees for interoperability and portability. He argued that ordering was not necessarily only a presentation concern, since it could affect how an algorithm processes shapes. He asked how equivalent portability guarantees could be achieved under the other options.

Bergi said option 1 illustrated how personal preferences could become normative requirements and argued that it would exclude valid use cases.

Robert said he preferred option 2. If SHACL UI uses `sh:order`, he believed the specification should define behaviour only where `sh:order` is explicitly present. He noted that an implementation might legitimately choose not to render shapes without `sh:order`.

Nick described an example involving a vocabulary profile where the same shape data may be rendered differently depending on the application. From his perspective, SHACL UI reduces the effort required to configure user interfaces. He said he was trying to better understand the portability use case and its target audience.

### Profiles and portability

Daniel said there was a gap in what could currently be expressed using SHACL. In his work, clients often use application profiles such as DCAT-AP, and he saw a need for a mechanism to override UI-related choices from an application profile without modifying that profile itself.

Robert said he understood Ieben's portability concern, particularly where customers might move from one vendor to another. He also noted that such transitions may currently be uncommon, but that the absence of an existing standard made the prevalence difficult to assess.

Bergi said the question came down to whether behaviour should be specified normatively or left to implementations. Given the limited time remaining, he felt the task force was trying to achieve too much and did not expect the wider Working Group to extend its schedule solely for SHACL UI.

Daniel agreed that the task force did not need to resolve every gap immediately. His preference was to address such gaps in the specification eventually, even if not in the current version.

Ieben said that anything placed in the specification should be clear and well-defined. If the group did not have enough time to define something properly, he preferred leaving the gap rather than adding weak normative statements that might create future backwards-compatibility problems.

Daniel said specifications are commonly referenced in tenders, which was one reason he favoured a small normative core.

Ieben summarised one argument from the presentation as: "Loosening later is cheap; tightening later breaks implementations."

Bergi said these concerns would be less relevant if the specification failed to gain adoption. He argued that the current approaches to `sh:order` and scoring would break use cases he had raised and reiterated his preference for building blocks and profiles.

### Use cases and scoring

Daniel asked for concrete use cases to be recorded rather than relying on general arguments. He asked Bergi to document the relevant use case in a GitHub issue so that the group could discuss it directly.

Daniel also referred to an earlier proposal from Bergi to support rendering without the scoring system. Daniel said that although he personally favoured more closed-world behaviour in some respects, he considered the open-world assumption important and believed the disagreement over whether scoring should be normative had reached a deadlock.

Bergi said he had already invested substantial effort documenting his concerns about the scoring system in a blog post and previous discussions, and felt those arguments had not been taken seriously. He questioned why he should create another issue and asked whether the task force was still pursuing the original direction of composable UI building blocks. He noted that this was the reason the Patterns section had originally been created.

Daniel said he and Ieben were not attempting to change the framing or pursue a separate agenda. He said his own goal was to avoid vendor lock-in and suggested that the task force should focus on use cases relevant to the Working Group and broader ecosystem rather than treating the discussion as one person's use cases against another's. He said he still had difficulty understanding Bergi's concrete use cases and believed the disagreement over building blocks and the normative core needed further discussion.

Bergi said he had already spent significant time documenting and discussing the issue and proposed that the decision be escalated to the Working Group.

Ieben said some of the statements in the discussion had become personal and asked participants to keep the discussion professional. He agreed with Daniel that the group should try to find common ground around enterprise use cases and avoiding vendor lock-in.

Nick said that, if the group could clearly define what it was testing, he believed a solution could support the relevant use cases. He gave an example of a profile with no inherent ordering requirements where an additional strict layout layer could nevertheless be useful. His own thinking had shifted towards allowing a stricter, testable system while still preserving agency for end users.

Daniel said he had read Bergi's blog post but remained unclear about the concrete use cases it was intended to address. He referred to home-automation and UDA-related examples and said he wanted more concrete use cases before treating those arguments as normative requirements.

Bergi said the blog post did contain the relevant use case and that similar use cases existed among several TopQuadrant customers. He said these were also similar to the UDA use cases.

Daniel asked whether an application profile was always necessary in those scenarios.

Bergi referred to a Netflix blog post and said its use case was similar to those of customers he worked with. He reiterated his proposal to use profiles and building blocks and asked what objection there was to supporting a strict profile alongside other approaches.

Ieben said the blog post was lengthy and that participants had limited time to search through external material for individual arguments. He asked that relevant arguments be brought directly into the GitHub issue so that the discussion and its provenance were recorded in one place.

Nick noted that the Profiles Task Force had experienced a similar problem, where use cases had not initially been written down because participants were relying on existing prior art.

Daniel noted that many SHACL UI use cases had similarly come from DASH.

Nick agreed that the Profiles Task Force had faced the same problem because substantial prior art already existed.

Daniel said he did not object to discussing profiles, but believed decisions should follow the W3C process and ultimately involve the wider Working Group where necessary. He also argued that the task force needed objective and broadly applicable use cases rather than relying primarily on one implementation or organisation's prior work.

Nick said disagreements of this kind were not unusual in Working Groups. He proposed that the co-chairs attend the following week's task force meeting and that, if the issue remained unresolved, it could then be brought to the wider Working Group.

Bergi clarified that Holger had only been mentioned as supporting evidence for his use case and again asked what the objection was to the profiles approach.

### Pull request process

Ieben asked how pull requests should be reviewed and merged. He said he did not believe he and Daniel were blocking progress and raised concerns about pull requests being marked as requiring changes without explanatory comments.

Bergi described a previous pull request where he had clearly stated during a call that he would not accept the proposed wording. He then went on leave, during which the pull request was approved and merged by Daniel and Ieben. Because of that experience, he had begun using blocking reviews to ensure he had enough time to review changes before they were merged. He said this was part of his responsibility as an editor and objected to being limited to a 24-hour review period.

Eliana agreed that reviewers needed sufficient time but said the group also needed to define how much review time was reasonable given the remaining schedule.

Daniel said the task force had previously discussed the limited time available. He, Ieben and Edmond had discussed a cleaner solution to an inconsistency involving the scoring algorithm. He argued that, if someone disagreed with the merged result, they could propose a follow-up pull request rather than using GitHub's review mechanism solely to prevent merging.

Ieben apologised to Bergi and said they had attempted to incorporate his comments. He said the intent had been to make progress and revise the text later where necessary.

Bergi said he had specifically objected to the use of `MUST`, that this objection was known before the merge, and that he should not then have been responsible for reopening the issue afterwards.

Ieben said Bergi had not commented directly in the Google Doc, although Edmond had, and that the material had been open for several weeks. He said the task force could not indefinitely wait for explicit approval from every editor before proceeding. He nevertheless apologised if the group had acted outside an agreed process.

Eliana encouraged participants to put their concerns in writing.

Robert recommended keeping substantive discussion in GitHub issues rather than separate documents to reduce the risk of comments being lost or overlooked.

Bergi agreed. He said feedback given during calls should also be acknowledged and recorded. He noted that the task force had previously moved discussion from a gist into Google Docs, but agreed that GitHub issues would be a better single location for future discussion.

**DECISION: Use only GitHub issues and pull requests for substantive task force discussion and review.**

Eliana encouraged the task force to negotiate reasonable review timelines, act in good faith and avoid taking disagreements personally.

Daniel acknowledged Bergi's earlier objection to `MUST`. He said that he, Ieben and Edmond had discussed revisiting the wording after issue #1083 was resolved. He supported establishing a formal process defining when pull requests could and could not be merged so that all participants were treated equally.

Bergi said that once wording entered the specification it became more difficult to remove. He recalled that Ieben had previously expressed a preference for using `MUST` broadly, although he could not substantiate the exact wording during the meeting.

Daniel said he did not believe that accurately reflected what he had said.

Robert noted that, in the Rules work, editorial notes had been added directly to the specification to record matters that needed to be revisited.
